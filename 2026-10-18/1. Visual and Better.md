As far as I can tell, there is a disconnect on the meaning of words such as "visual", "better", etc. and that's what I'm probably reacting to. I read "...text is better than visual..." as a blanket statement instead of as a qualified statement "...text is better than visual for this use case..."

- Drakon is a better flow charting notation than the other more familiar concept of flowcharts. I doubt (but don't know for sure) that Microsoft incorporates Drakon-like concepts into its technology

- Common concepts, like state, control flow, mutation, feedback, passage of time are so clumsy to express as text that we've simply expunged these concepts, whole-hog from sight, instead of switching to different notations for those use-cases (anecdote: I drew a simple 3-bubble state machine to represent a simple control flow concept, but needed some 20 lines of Python to express the same concept)

- Our use of text was severely affected by the 500-year old invention of Gutenberg's printing press (typesetting). For example, one use of the vinculum was replaced by the harder-to-read use of parentheses only for technical reasons (typesetting).

- Our use of text is already augmented by the use of visual aspects. Machines don't care about spaces and indentation, only humans do.
