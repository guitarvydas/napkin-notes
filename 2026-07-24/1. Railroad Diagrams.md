I believe that you should be able to go the other way, too, diagrams to text - and that this would be very useful. In fact someone at HMN asked me if I knew of a tool that would transpile railroad _diagrams_ to _textual_ grammars. I use drawio (drawio.com) to save diagrams in .drawio format (a derivative of graphML which is derived from XML). I use OhmJS to parse the XML and to discard the graphics-only details (about 90% of the data). Then I use a combination of OhmJS and shell-outs to SWIPL (a Prolog, relational programming) to infer enough information from the diagram to produce code (in my case Python, but I imagine that producing .ohm would be just as easy). I don't actually _need_ to use Prolog, but it's a lot easier than writing inferencing loops in something functional or imperative, like Javascript, typescript, Python, etc.  XML, SVG, .drawio are extremely parseable with OhmJS. If interested, I can describe the techniques further and give concrete examples.
