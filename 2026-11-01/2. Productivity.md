# Kinds of Productivity
2026-11-01

 I value extrapolative productivity (thinking outside of the box) more than interpolative productivity (more nuance, more features, more edge-cases, "we've always done it this way", notation worship, epicycles).

What we think of as programming today, is what I would call Production Engineering - optimization, detailing. There is, also, (Design) Engineering - iterating and getting a design right before wasting time on making it "better". We have been emphasizing Production Engineering and essentially ignoring Design. Prematurely declaring that a problem is going to be solved in a "functional" manner is most often a commitment towards wasting time right out of the gate.

I don't think of Windows clocking in at 55MLOC as a win. It would be better to do away with operating systems altogether (quite possible) rather than adding more features and edge-cases to them.

AHA insights are great strides forward, not just mere nuances on top of existing technology. AHA moments in early code written in assembler: \

- lisp -- garbage collection, first-class functions, structure transforming macros (very different from C-like macros). This kind of macro actually came a few years later, but relied on Lispiness. Tunney's more modern Sector Lisp does GC even better.\
 
- UNIX -- creation of an operating system better than OS/360 and bare monitors, running on a mini-computer (PDP-7), pipes (written in assembler in V3, preceding the V4 rewrite in C of UNIX), stolen ideas from Multics, but drastically shrunk-down\
 
- Turbo Pascal --small, quick language compiler running on tiny machines\

- Visicalc -- invention/popularization of interactive spreadsheet idea for desktop machines\

- Sketchpad -- Sketchpad is a constraint-based system. Alan Kay once asked Ivan Sutherland how he managed to build the world's first interactive computer graphics system, non-procedural programming language, and object-oriented software system all by himself in just one year.  The common belief is that people like this are, somehow, just massively smarter than everyone else, but, one would think that at least some PhDs since then were smart, they'd just been misled on how to approach solving problems.\

Surely we've had AHA moments using function-based languages, but, IMO, many fewer AHAs per unit of time spent.

Forcing our thought process through a function-based funnel causes lack of inventiveness - which I equate to being a lack of productivity.
