# Responsibility

2026-11-09

## Professional Engineer 

- guarantee: specific guarantees of operation
- penalty: lawsuit resulting in financial settlement or jail time

## Hardware company

- goal: zero defects in the field
- guarantee: specific guarantees of operation
- penalty:
	- expensive repairs to products, already shipped, in the field, to upgrade hardware
	- loss of business (the invisible hand of the market)

## Software nonprofessional engineers and cottage industry

- EULA - disown responsibility
- effectively no concept of zero defects in the field
- guarantee: practically none
- penalty:
	- possible expense of deflecting lawsuits
	- historical "marketing costs" to convince customers that this is just "software" and that they should just put up with what they get
	- possible loss of business, albeit hindered by dearth of serious competition
- continuous push for more new features without understanding of how they interact, and too many featuritis edge-cases tangled up with implicit synchronization to thoroughly test

# How?

## Professional Engineers

Professional Engineers are trained in "best practices" in university and are legally certified (certification exams some years after graduation).

## Hardware

Hardware producers employ various kinds of testing: 

- incoming inspection
- design reviews for testability
- outgoing test and ATE (Automated Test Equipment)
- employ QA department to ferret out design inadequacies and implementation inadequacies (bugs). 

Hardware production design involves avoiding single-sourced components.

Features limited to those that can be guaranteed, pass QA and outgoing testing.

Hardware, unlike present-day software, is based on massive parallelism and isolated, stand-alone components.

## Software Cottage industry

Weasel out of responsibility by creating walls of legalese and EULAs. 

Weasel out of proper design by relying on CD - Continuous Deployment - leaving design feedback in the hands of paying customers, however slowly this feedback occurs. 

Reliance on essentially a religious belief in the yet unrealized abilities of mathematics and the computational approach to remove the need for serious testing, i.e. testing of the *design* in addition to testing of code[^1]. 

[^1]: See [Design of Everyday Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Design_of_Everyday_Things) by Norman for examples of lack of good design leading to personal injury and death.

Fundamental inability to use isolated, testable software components due to the reliance on a synchronous / sequential approach. Hidden - implicit synchronization - dependencies occur due to the overuse of the synchronous approach and on the historical reliance on memory sharing. A *tell* of overuse of memory-sharing is the old-fashioned worry about "thread safety".

The advent of AI coding will likely make matters worse, due to a software culture that avoids serious, full-spectrum testing and isn't held legally accountable.
