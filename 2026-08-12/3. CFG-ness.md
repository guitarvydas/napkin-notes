> What surprised me a little here was suggestion of prototyping with PEGs. In theory the order-dependency of the rules makes it hard to know what CFG one is prototyping, in my mind at least. Do you not find this to be a problem in practice?

Interesting comment. I agree. OTOH CFG-iness was forced down our throats based on then-current biases about "efficiency" (based on 20th century biases influenced by 20th century hardware). PEG let's us escape that constraint. I think that that is a good thing and, hence, am more cavalier with my opinions of PEG and what else you can do with it (I use OhmJS in ways that I would never do with CFG-based parser tools).

---

1) PEG is top-down whereas most of the LR(k) stuff we've become used to is bottom-up. You can say things in PEG, like "match (, then skip everything in between, then match )", which is extremely painful to say with LR.
2) I wrote a lot of Lisp macros. I use OhmJS to write macros for text languages.
