# AI Slop vs. Artistry

Over the years, I've heard something similar to the current hype about AI. From my perspective, the same pattern is simply repeating.

I used to be very interested in songwriting and in music production. When drum machines and DAWs and auto-tune and music videos came out, more people could create music, but most of it sounded like slop — degraded versions of real, artistic music.

The slop factor turned out to be due to the masses playing with the new media and accepting the first thing that could be created. When actual artists figured out how to work with the new medium, the quality of their results no longer sounded like slop.

This is probably where we are with AI. A lot more people are now able to create music, images, and prose that resembles what went before. On the other hand, "real artists" haven't yet come to grips on how to manipulate and work with this new medium. Artists evoke feelings in us. Slop generators tend to create formulaic products that don't really touch us.

## How I use it

I use AI — Claude, and Leanpub's ghostAI — to write blogs and articles and chapters for books. I've been doing this for a few years. I think that I'm getting better at it. It's helping me convert my point-form notes into readable text with less effort than it used to take.

Claude reads my writing and criticizes it from a writing perspective. Claude acts like a brainstorming co-writer for me. I feed my point-form notes to Claude and don't expect to accept the first draft. I bounce ideas off of it. I work with it and criticize and refine the draft as I would with a human co-writer, though, maybe with fewer arguments.

I typically go around the loop three times. I write a set of ideas in point form. I ask Claude to draft an article. I read the article and keep a note-taking window on the side. As I read and react to passages and to actual content, I jot down notes in the side window. When I hit the end of the draft, I copy/paste my notes into the chat and wait for Claude's revised version of the draft. I do this about three times, until I have no notes and comments to make about the revisions.

## How I'd apply this to songwriting

If I wanted to apply this to songwriting, I would use what I learned from Pat Pattison for lyric writing. I would want to cast the "rules" that I learned from Pattison into AI "learnings." I wouldn't stop on the first go-around. I would interact with the AI and gradually tune the result to fit my own artistic taste.

I would do something similar with melody and music writing. Go around a few times, be inspired by the co-writer, and suggest new variations.

## AI is only a tool

AI should help an artist arrive at one's artistic vision faster. AI is only a tool, like a typewriter, or a junior co-writer.
