Forth is geared for specialization, modern programming languages are geared for generalization and nuance and complexity. Two very different goals. If you want to build a small-footprint, opcode-based something for non-programmers, the Forth attitude is more appropriate. If you want to production engineer a piece of code to make it completely self-consistent (for the goals of the programmer, instead of to the end-user) by expending memory footprint, then GPLs (general-purpose programming languages) are appropriate. As I understand it, the Forth camp split into two distinct camps - some tried to (over-)generalize Forth, but, in the traditional camp, Moore opposed this kind of thing. A colleague who is more into Forth than I am, also loved the smallness of Rebol (which apparently inspired the invention of JSON), and is now using Janet. (My view: it is better to think in terms of design stages, use specialized tools to build and try out MVIs (minimum viable implementation), then production-engineer the heck out of it with generalized PLs (building code twice is faster than building it just once)).
