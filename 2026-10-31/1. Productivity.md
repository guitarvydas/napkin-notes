One form of productivity concerns itself with ideas that extrapolate beyond the current state of the art. Another form of productivity is concerned with interpolation - cramming more features and edge-cases into ideas.
- lisp -- garbage collection, first-class functions, structure transforming macros (very different from C-like macros) (this kind of macros came later, but relied on Lispiness)
- UNIX -- operating system better than OS/360 and monitors, running on a mini-computer (PDP-7), pipes (written in assembler in V3, preceding C version UNIX V4), stolen ideas from Multics, shrunk-down
- Turbo Pascal --small, quick language compiler running on small machines
- Visicalc -- invention/popularization of interactive spreadsheet idea for desktop machines
- Sketchpad -- Alan Kay once asked Ivan Sutherland how he managed to build the world's first interactive computer graphics system, non-procedural programming language, and object-oriented software system all by himself in just one year.  Sketchpad is a constraint-based system
