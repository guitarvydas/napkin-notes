Here's what I found, in rough order of importance.

**Claims worth tightening**

- **"Engineering" as a legal term.** Your hedge ("It is my understanding") is appropriate. In Ontario, "Professional Engineer" and the practice of engineering are regulated under the Professional Engineers Act, and licensed engineers stamp work and carry professional liability. Software is a gray area, though: most people with "software engineer" on their business card are not licensed. Since you're in Toronto, you may want to say "in Ontario" or "in Canada" rather than implying this is universal. Otherwise a reader will say "my title is Software Engineer and I've never stamped anything."
- **The blame rules.** "If it breaks for other reasons, it's probably the Architects' fault" is a catch-all that reads as a joke or as an overreach. If you mean it seriously, one sentence of justification would help (e.g., requirements and environment fit are the Architects' job). If not, soften it.
- **The building analogy.** In real construction, architects are also licensed and liable, and the structural engineer works under or alongside them. Your mapping is reasonable, but a pedantic reader may object that architects aren't the "fuzzy, nobody's liable" tier. Saying "loosely" would help.
- **"CD (of CI/CD)."** It's unclear whether you mean continuous delivery or deployment. The point lands better if you say "continuous deployment as a substitute for getting it right before shipping."

**Consistency and clarity**

- Abbreviations drift: "SW Architecture," "SW Engineers," then "S Engineers and S Architects" in the QA bullet. That "S" is probably a typo for "SW."
- "MVI (Minimum Viable Implementation)" will be read as a typo for MVP unless you contrast them. Either way, introduce it as your own term if it is one.
- "Q/A" is usually written "QA."
- "milque-toast" is "milquetoast."
- "Real professions ≡ split work along these lines" and "Cottage industry ≡ ..." use ≡ in a way that reads oddly in prose. "Real professions split work along these lines" would be cleaner. Same for the second one.
- The long parenthetical in the last sentence is hard to parse. Consider splitting it: cottage industries produce milquetoast results, poor reproducibility, and unbalanced design versus implementation. They also lean on customers for feedback, using continuous deployment in place of striving for zero field defects.
- "Software Production Engineering" may be confused with production operations or SRE. "Performance engineering" or "Optimization" might be clearer, unless the term is deliberate.
- "Software Analysis: proving provenance" is the least clear bullet. "Traceability from blueprints to code" might communicate it faster.
- QA and Test Engineering overlap a bit. A line distinguishing them (QA tests the product adversarially, Test Engineering makes the product testable) would help.

- You may want to either delete the "???" bullet or turn it into a short sentence. A few candidates you may have missed: requirements/product management, security, operations/maintenance, and documentation.
