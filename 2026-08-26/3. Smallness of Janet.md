That implies that there's a lot of extra fluff in "compiled" executables that Janet doesn't bother to use. The implication is that "compiling" everything is wasteful. I conclude that "general purpose" means "fat". Why don't all compilers compile to a smaller VM and then allow spot-unrolling to gain speed where it is actually needed? Does Janet provide hooking to "native" routines or provide "#asm" directives?

One of the (unrecognized) aspects of S/SL (syntax/semantic language - a compiler-building tool) is that the programmer defines a set of operations and S/SL compiles to bytecodes _only_ for those operations. The logic is expressed in terms of the bytecoded operations, the implementation of the bytecodes and bytecode case statement is done with compiled code (Pascal, C).

I think that most of the meat in an app doesn't need to be compiled down to actual opcodes. Only a small part(s) of any app needs to be optimized like that. The heavy parts of the logic swamp out any overhead due to bytecode indirection.
