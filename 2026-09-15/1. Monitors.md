FWIW, I used monitors a lot in Concurrent Euclid.

FWIW, more recently, I've come to several new/different conclusions:
1. Monitors are just a complicated expression of servers that employ private memory. Signals are basically just message sends between servers.
2. Concurrency is not concurrent in the sense of "running at the same time" (asynchronous). What we call "concurrency" is derived from old-fashioned "time-sharing" and is more like interleaving than actual async concurrency. The sequential/synchronous paradigm (e.g. functional, function-based) can _never_ express actual async concurrency (functions are synchronous by definition). The main wart in so-called "concurrency" is the assumption that memory is shared (an idea inherited from the 20th century and ultra-expensive CPUs). For example, nodes on the internet don't share memory, hence, trying to express internet-y concepts using current programming languages results in unexpected gotchas like callback hell (and, earlier, the Mars Pathfinder fiasco).
3. [Statecharts](https://guitarvydas.github.io/2023/11/27/Statecharts-Papers-We-Love-Video.html) - to my eyes - are a much less complicated expression of concurrency (superseded by recursive, electronics-like schematics, that I now call PBP).
